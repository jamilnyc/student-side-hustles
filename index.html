Rules to know first

  • Working papers: New York requires employment certificates for most jobs under 18. Babysitting and casual yard work or household chores (age 14+) are exempt. Working for a business, even part-time, generally needs papers from your school.
  • No power equipment: The chores exemption excludes power-driven machinery. Skip mowers, blowers and the like.
  • Not allowed under 18: Exterior building cleaning (like window washing) is on the prohibited list. Never use ladders for customers.
  • No street-corner selling: Selling goods or services on a public sidewalk needs a city general vendor license, which are capped with long waitlists. Work at a customer's home, building or with their permission instead.
  • Apps are 18+: Most gig and marketplace apps require adults. A parent has to create the account.

Land your first customers

  1. Start close to home. Neighbors, relatives’ friends, your building’s group chat, your place of worship or school community.
  2. Make a simple flyer. First name, a parent’s phone number, 3 services, one line about you. Post in lobbies only with permission.
  3. Do one job really well. Show up on time, send a quick text when finished, and ask for one referral.
  4. Raise prices gradually. After a few happy repeat customers, add $2–5.

Stay safe

  • Tell a parent where you’ll be and when you’ll be done.
  • Don’t go into a stranger’s home alone. Meet in lobbies or work outside, or bring a friend.
  • Work in pairs for anything physical, and lift with your legs.
  • Get paid when the job is done and agree on the price first.
  • Cold-weather work strains the body. Take breaks while shoveling.
